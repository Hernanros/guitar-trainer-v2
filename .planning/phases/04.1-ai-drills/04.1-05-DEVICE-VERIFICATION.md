### Why the rebuild — SPENT, 2026-09-16

The `0db7955` pair was superseded. **Item 2 would have failed at ~60s on those builds for a
client-side reason that has nothing to do with the server.**

FLE-29 (`12c63fb`) landed after the server fixes and is a **mobile** fix. On iOS, RN's fetch is the
whatwg-fetch polyfill: it registers `xhr.ontimeout` but never assigns `xhr.timeout`, so the native
request keeps `defaultSessionConfiguration`'s **60s** `timeoutIntervalForRequest` — below the
**72.1s** a real breakdown takes. The symptom is easy to misfile: the first tap fails at ~60s while
the server succeeds at ~75s and caches it, the second tap succeeds instantly from cache, and **two of
the three weekly quota units are already spent**. That looks like an item-2 failure plus an item-5
quota anomaly when it is one client bug. `apiFetch` now routes the breakdown call through
XMLHttpRequest with an explicit 240s timeout — above the server's 150s ceiling on purpose, so the
server's structured error is what the user sees rather than a client abort. Android was unaffected
(OkHttp defaults to no timeout).

### FLE-5 D9, 2026-09-16 — the app now measures its own timing, so read the numbers

**Replaces the "second metronome app" method above.** Do not phase-compare two apps by ear. They are

| What you hear | What it actually is | Whose problem |
|---|---|---|
| Slowly slides out of phase | Accumulated grid error | Scheduler bug — a real defect |
| Beats wobble, phase holds | Per-beat JS-thread jitter | Expected; the question is how much |
| Click stops, then resumes | The OS suspended the app | Wake lock / background audio |

The engine has been computing the exact figure per beat since its first commit; nothing displayed
it. Now it does.

**How to open it: long press the tempo readout** (the "120 BPM / From this drill" block on the right
of the metronome card). Hidden by default so the drill screen stays a drill screen. Long press


**What each number means, and what fails:**

| Field | Reading | Fail looks like |
|---|---|---|
| `grid` | Where the latest beat sits against `anchor + n × interval`. **This is the "holds tempo" number.** It does NOT accumulate — it should look the same at 45 minutes as at 30 seconds. | Growing steadily with time. A grid of ±20ms at 45 min is a pass; ±4000ms is the scheduler failing. |
| `jitter max` / `max jitter % of a beat` | Worst single beat. This is what the hardware is *for* — no laptop can measure it. | Record it regardless. Under ~1% of a beat is inaudible to anyone; if it is audible, the percentage is the number to report. |
| `skipped` | Beats the grid contained that the device never fired — counted from gaps in the beat index. **Non-zero means the app was suspended or the JS thread stalled**, not that timing is bad. | Any non-zero value during a run where the screen stayed on. 30s of iOS auto-lock reads as ~59 skipped at 120 BPM. |
| `dropped` | Clicks swallowed for arriving more than half a beat late, so they were not played out of position. | A handful over 45 min is the design working. Dozens means the thread is badly contended. |
| run clock / beats | Sanity check that it ran as long as you think. | — |

**Diagnosed and filed as FLE-57 (Kaya).** `audioEmitter.ts`: `play()` is a synchronous JSI call,
