## 2. §4 verdict — did the duration vocabulary and `Tab.feel` move FLE-102's finding?

**Yes, decisively — and it introduced a worse defect in exact proportion.**

FLE-102's finding (T9): 58 of 72 bars monotone; the whole corpus used only **three**
duration values (`quarter` 218, `eighth` 308, `half` 6); no triplets, sixteenths,
dotted values, rests or ties in 532 notes; every shuffle written as straight eighths.

I re-measured the baseline rather than quoting it: `04.1-07-EVAL-CONFIRM-RAW.txt`
contains exactly three duration values (`quarter` 132, `eighth` 68, `half` 13) and
14 of its 16 drills carry uniform durations (`e0` in the grader's dimension line).
FLE-102's T9 reproduces numerically.

In this run the verifier's `UNIFORM_DURATION` warning — the codified form of T9 —
fires on **9 of ~26 observable main-tab bars (35%)**, against the baseline's **81%**:

| song | meter Sonnet chose | monotone bars | METER defects |
|---|---|---|---|
| Lenny | **6/8** | 0 / 8 | 7 |
| Little Wing | **12/8** | 0 / 6 | 8 |
| Beat It | 4/4 | 1 / 4 | 4 |
| Wonderwall | 4/4 | **8 / 8** | **0** |

**The correlation is perfectly inverse, and it is the whole story.** The songs that
adopted the new vocabulary are exactly the songs that now fail METER. The one song
that stayed on the old uniform-eighth grid — Wonderwall, 8/8 bars monotone — is the
only one with zero METER defects. Its 2 failures are unrelated (OWN_CHORDS).

Supporting evidence that the vocabulary is genuinely in use: the bad bar sums are
non-integer in ways only the new values can produce — observed sums include
**3.33333** (= 10/3, i.e. `triplet-eighth` at 1/3 beat), 3.5, 4.5, 5.5 and 8. The
baseline could not have produced 3.33333 at all; it had no triplet value.

So: **T9 is largely solved. T10 ("bars that do not add up", which FLE-102 measured
at 3 songs / 6 bars) has regressed into the dominant failure mode at 19 defects.**
The prompt taught Sonnet a richer rhythmic vocabulary without teaching it to make
the bar still sum to its time signature.

The clearest instance is Little Wing. Sonnet chose **12/8** — musically right for the
song — and then filled the bars with `triplet-eighth` values. In 12/8 the triplet
subdivision *is already the meter*, so writing triplets inside it double-counts the
subdivision: all six bars sum to 3.33333 where 12/8 wants 6. This is conceptually the
same error FLE-118 §3's C8 double-applied-swing check was built to catch, but it
arrives as a METER sum failure rather than as C8, so C8 never fires on it.

`_check_meter` uses exact `Fraction` arithmetic with no tolerance (FLE-115 §2.2), and
`time_signature_beats` correctly wants 3 for 6/8 and 6 for 12/8. I verified the
verifier before attributing the failures: **the meter math is right and the model is
wrong.**

## 5. Two measurement defects found while grading

1. **`grade_eval.py` conflates "no output" with "bad output."** An errored song scores
   8×FAIL, so a refused breakdown is indistinguishable from one that emitted eight bad
   drills. It also moves the denominator (40 vs the baseline's 35, where L4 was N/A on
   all 5 songs). An errored song should score N/A — *unexercised* — the way FLE-123
   already reasons about N/A gates. Without this, "0/40" overstates what was measured.

2. **A misleading log line.** `breakdown.py:1070` logs `"passed verification with N
   warning(s)"` whenever warnings exist, including on breakdowns that are refused
   microseconds later by the blocking branch at `:1074`. Every song here logs "passed"
   and then fails. One `verify_tab` call, two log lines that read as two verdicts.

## 6. Governor accounting: failed calls are not recorded against the ceiling

The dev DB held 437 rows / $49.0567 before this run and 438 rows / $49.3951 after —
**+1 row for 5 paid calls.** Four completed Anthropic calls (~$0.53 of real spend) left
no `governor_calls` row at all; the single new row is Wonderwall's reservation.

The reservation row is written at dispatch and rolled back when the breakdown raises,
so **every verification-failed call is invisible to `pilot_spend()`** and does not
count against the $25 ceiling. The money is spent regardless. My per-song invocation
is what exposed this: in the dead 2026-10-25T23:35 run, Lenny's row *did* persist
(with `dollars_actual` 0.128520) because a later song's commit flushed it — in a
one-song process there is no later commit, so it vanishes. This is the same family as
FLE-140 ("end the transaction on every refusal path in `_reserve_call_slot`") and is
worth its own card: a budget guardrail that under-counts exactly the calls most likely
to be retried is the wrong way to be wrong.

Costs in the table above therefore come from the script's own printed
`Estimated cost` lines, not from the DB.
