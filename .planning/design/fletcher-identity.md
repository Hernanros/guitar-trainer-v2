## The name — and the twist

The app is called **Fletcher** as a reference to Terence Fletcher, the jazz teacher played by JK Simmons in *Whiplash* (2014). The demanding-teacher archetype. "Not my tempo."

**But this Fletcher is not that Fletcher.**

The whole joke — and the whole product positioning — sits in that gap. The name promises Fletcher-cruelty and the app delivers the opposite:

> **The teacher Fletcher should have been.**

The Whiplash character had the luxury of hunting savants — Neiman was a once-in-a-generation talent Fletcher could afford to break because he'd rebuild into something extraordinary. **Our users are not savants.** They are working guitarists — intermediate and advanced players who improve through the grind. Real practice, done consistently, over long stretches, without a genius-teacher watching. The daily grind is the mechanism of improvement.

The app respects that grind. It reinforces it. It celebrates hard-won achievements *because* they were hard-won — not because we're handing out participation trophies, but because the work was real and the win was earned. Fletcher-the-name signals "we take music seriously here." Fletcher-the-app is the coach who takes *you* seriously.

The joke lands harder because of the contrast. Anyone who's seen the film gets it in five seconds. Anyone who hasn't seen the film just sees a well-named app with a coach who's in their corner.

## Voice & tone — the actual rules

- **Reinforcing, not critical.** The default response to a session is recognition. Correction only when the user explicitly asks or when structured feedback is warranted (Phase 3+).
- **Celebrate what was earned.** A three-day streak on a hard chord change is a real win. Say so, mean it, don't undercut it.
- **Direct + warm.** Terse copy is fine; cold copy is not. "Nailed it." beats "Great job!" — both are positive, one has a spine.
- **Respect the grind.** The user is doing hard, boring work daily. Copy should acknowledge that this is not easy and the payoff is not automatic.
- **No fake enthusiasm.** No emojis in Fletcher's voice (system errors and playful moments can use them sparingly). No exclamation points except at genuine peak moments (streak-broken celebration, breakthrough on a hard technique). Never write "Amazing!!" or "You're crushing it!" — Fletcher would never say that even in warm mode.
- **Second person, present or perfect tense.** "You held the shuffle." not "Session complete: shuffle held."
- **Believe in the user.** The subtext of every message: you can do this, the work is paying off, keep going.
- **Never sarcastic at the user.** Sarcasm about the app itself (empty states, error copy) is fine.

**The vocabulary is the signature; the follow-up is the promise.**

Real-Fletcher used sharp musical vocabulary as a weapon and walked out of the room. Our-Fletcher uses the *same vocabulary* — it's accurate diagnosis, real musician terminology — and then stays in the room to coach the next step. The joke is that our-Fletcher looks like Fletcher on the surface (same terse feedback words) and is fundamentally different underneath (backs every diagnosis with a path forward).

Same three syllables. Completely different relationship.

- Real-Fletcher: `"Rushing."` [walks away]
- Our-Fletcher: `"Rushing. Metronome to 100 tomorrow — you'll get it."`

The vocabulary also serves a real teaching function. "Rushing" and "Dragging" are terms grinders learn to hear in themselves. Using them consistently in the app teaches that vocabulary the same way a real teacher would.

Contrast — what Fletcher-the-app should NEVER say:

- ❌ "You're doing amazing! 🎉🎸" (fake, over-punctuated, no spine)
- ❌ "Not everyone can play E7. Don't feel bad." (condescending)
- ❌ Anything that implies natural talent — "Some people just have it" is antithetical to the whole positioning
- ❌ Fletcher-vocabulary *without* the follow-up ("Rushing." on its own is real-Fletcher; the warmth is in the coaching that follows)
- ❌ "Great job!" (generic praise; Fletcher wouldn't say it and neither would a real teacher)

What Fletcher-the-app *should* say (draft vocabulary, tune in Phase 2 copy iteration):

**Diagnosis + coaching (the signature pattern — sharp word, then the next step):**
- ✅ `"Rushing. Metronome to 100 tomorrow — you'll get it."`
- ✅ `"Dragging. Try tapping the beat with your foot next round."`
- ✅ `"Not my tempo. Slow the whole thing down 10 bpm and rebuild."` — Fletcher's signature line, delivered as coaching rather than dismissal
- ✅ `"Do it again. You've got it in you — one more pass."` — "Do it again" from real-Fletcher is a bark; from our-Fletcher it's earned confidence in the user

**Earned praise (rare and specific):**
- ✅ `"That's the tempo."` — the highest compliment Fletcher-vocabulary allows, reserved for real clean sessions
- ✅ `"You held it."` — recognition of maintaining time under pressure
- ✅ `"Cleaner than yesterday."` — progress-aware praise, references the grind
- ✅ `"Nailed the shuffle."` — specific technique landed
- ✅ `"Three days on E7 → A7. That's real."` — streak recognition, honors the daily work
- ✅ `"110 bpm on Sweet Home Chicago. You couldn't do that two weeks ago."` — historical contrast, biggest wins

**Acknowledgment (when the day was hard):**
- ✅ `"That was a grind. Show up tomorrow."` — respects effort without judging outcome
- ✅ `"Off day. Reset with the metronome at 90 tomorrow."` — no shame, offers a concrete restart
- ✅ `"Welcome back. Pick up where you left off."` — after a missed streak, no scolding

Notice the pattern: **sharp diagnosis → specific next step**. Never sharp diagnosis alone; never empty praise; never generic warmth. Fletcher-vocabulary carries authority; the coaching that follows carries the warmth.

## Where the joke lands, and where the warmth lands

The Fletcher *reference* (name, aesthetic, high-standards vibe) and the Fletcher *voice* (warm coach, reinforcing) live in different places:

### The joke — the reference lands here

**Onboarding "meet your teacher" moment:**
> "Meet Fletcher. He's the teacher Fletcher should have been."
> [image: stylized coach character — see asset direction below]
> [continue]

This is where the joke pays off. Anyone who gets the reference gets a smile. Anyone who doesn't gets a clear positioning ("your teacher") without needing to know the film.

**Splash screen tagline:**
Options — pick one during Phase 2 UI-phase iteration:
- "The teacher Fletcher should have been." (explicit callback; only works if the user has already seen the onboarding line)
- "Every day. In tempo. Together." (Fletcher-adjacent phrasing without the callback; warmer)
- No tagline, just the logo (safest, ships with any icon)

"Not my tempo." *can* work as the splash tagline — it's the signature Fletcher-vocabulary — but only if the app has established elsewhere (onboarding, first session) that our-Fletcher backs it up with coaching. If the splash is a user's first-ever impression, "Not my tempo." reads as intimidating rather than iconic. Best to defer the tagline decision to Phase 2 UI-phase after the onboarding voice is nailed down.

**Empty states + placeholder screens:**
Fletcher-flavored but warm.
- Library (Phase 5 placeholder): `"Library is coming. For now, today's song is enough."` (calm, reassuring, not "coming soon")
- Toolkit (Phase 5 placeholder): `"Metronome and tuner coming. Bring your own for now."` (matter-of-fact, respects the user's competence)
- Offline banner: `"You're offline. Today's session is here."` (helpful, no drama)
- Missed practice: `"Welcome back. Pick up where you left off."` (no shame; forgives the miss)

### The warmth — the voice lands here

**Session rating buttons** (Phase 3):
Full Fletcher-vocabulary as the rating options. The user self-reports how the session went using the actual musical terminology — this both lands the joke *and* teaches the vocabulary. The buttons should include both wins and honest problems; the app treats the "honest problem" ratings without judgment (they're diagnostic input for the skill graph, not confessions of failure).

Rough vocabulary (tune in Phase 3 with real users):

**Wins:**
- **"That's the tempo"** — session was clean; user is confident it landed
- **"Nailed it"** — technique-specific win (the chord change, the shuffle, whatever the session was about)
- **"Held it"** — steady session; time and technique were maintained under pressure

**Honest problems (Fletcher-vocabulary as self-report, not judgment):**
- **"Rushing"** — user knows they pushed tempo
- **"Dragging"** — user knows they fell behind the beat
- **"Not my tempo"** — user couldn't lock in the target tempo at all
- **"Do it again"** — user wants another pass tomorrow; nothing landed today

The last four are honest musician-self-report — the user IS the one saying "I was rushing." The app didn't say it about them. This is important: the user gets to use the vocabulary as their own diagnostic language. The app then follows with the coaching next step (see session summary micro-copy below).

None of the "honest problem" ratings implies failure. They feed the skill graph the same way any other rating does — as "this technique needs another pass" — and the follow-up copy from Fletcher makes that framing explicit.

**Session summary micro-copy** (Phase 3):
After a session, one line from Fletcher acknowledging what happened. Options templated by what the user rated — the follow-up is where the coaching (and the warmth) lives. Real-Fletcher would say the diagnosis and walk out; our-Fletcher says the diagnosis and stays.

Response templates by rating:

- After **"That's the tempo"**: `"Same time tomorrow. Push the bpm 5 next session."` — validates the win, sets the next challenge
- After **"Nailed it"**: `"Nailed. You're getting cleaner on the transitions."` — echoes back, notes the progress
- After **"Held it"**: `"Solid. Same routine tomorrow, same tempo."` — recognizes steady work
- After **"Rushing"**: `"Rushing. Metronome to 100 tomorrow — you'll get it."` — the signature pattern; diagnosis then next step
- After **"Dragging"**: `"Dragging. Tap the beat with your foot tomorrow — feel it first, then play."` — same pattern, different fix
- After **"Not my tempo"**: `"Slow the whole thing 10 bpm and rebuild tomorrow. You'll be back."` — reframes as a temporary reset, not failure
- After **"Do it again"**: `"Do it again. Tomorrow's the same song — you've heard it now."` — acknowledges the pass isn't done, keeps forward motion

Notice each response follows the pattern: **acknowledge (often echoing the user's word) → concrete instruction for tomorrow → subtle confidence signal**. That's the whole voice in three beats.

**Streak / achievement copy** (Phase 3, Phase 4):
When a real earned achievement lands, mark it. Suggested moments:
- 7-day streak: `"Seven days. That's habit."`
- First clean session on a new technique: `"You've got the E7 → A7 change now."`
- Tempo increase on an established piece: `"110 bpm on Sweet Home Chicago. You couldn't do that two weeks ago."`
- Skill graph node moves from "working" to "reliable": `"E-shape barre chords are reliable now. On to the next thing."`

Rules for these:
- **Specific.** Reference the exact skill, tempo, or duration.
- **Historical.** When possible, contrast with past state ("couldn't do that two weeks ago").
- **Forward-looking.** Every celebration hints at what's next — no dead ends.
- **Rare enough to matter.** If every session gets a "Nailed it!" the word means nothing. Reserve strong praise for moments that earned it.

## Asset direction

**Character:** stylized coach, not-quite-Simmons.

Prompt direction for AI-generated character (POC through v1.0):
> *"Portrait of a music teacher in his early 50s, bald, black turtleneck, seated slightly forward with elbows on knees, warm-but-focused expression, direct eye contact with viewer, dim jazz-club amber lighting, painterly stylized illustration, contemporary editorial feel."*

Key differences from my previous draft:
- **Warm-but-focused expression, not glaring.** The character is present, not intimidating.
- **Seated forward with elbows on knees.** Body language of a coach listening, not a threat.
- **Turtleneck, not tie or dress shirt.** Softens the corporate/teacher-in-suit read.
- **Direct eye contact.** He's here for you, watching. Not judging — attending.

Alternatives if AI gen doesn't land:
- **Silhouette approach**: profile silhouette + acoustic guitar in the background. Reference without face. Warm, legally safest.
- **Iconographic**: a single acoustic guitar on a stool under a spotlight. No character at all; the coaching is implied by the "everything is set up for you" vibe.

**App icon (deferred):**
Not a face. The face at 60px doesn't read as anything specific. Better options when we tackle it:
- Warm-toned "F" monogram in a strong serif
- Guitar silhouette against a warm gradient
- A single fretboard fragment showing an E7 chord shape

Design at 1024px, verify at 60px, verify on both light and dark home screens.

## Legal / IP caveats

- JK Simmons' likeness is his property. Nothing this document proposes uses his actual likeness in a commercial product. The character is Fletcher-*adjacent*, deliberately distinct in expression, posture, and coloring.
- "Fletcher" as a name is a common surname, not trademarked. Safe to use.
- Direct quotes from *Whiplash* — short musical-jargon phrases ("Not my tempo", "Rushing", "Dragging") are ordinary music-teacher vocabulary, not protected phrases on their own. Safe to use as UI copy. Do NOT use longer signature lines from the film ("were you rushing or were you dragging" as a full quote, the "Charlie Parker" monologue, etc.) — those are film-specific enough to carry copyright concern.
- Never ship film stills.

## Product-level principle underneath all this

**We are not building an app for savants. We are building an app for grinders.**

Grinders show up every day. They practice the same three-chord progression for forty minutes because it isn't clean yet. They get frustrated. They plateau. Eventually — through pure repetition — they become someone who can play the piece.

The app's job is to make that daily grind survivable, to notice the small wins the grinder can't see (bpm crept up 3, transition got cleaner, three-day streak), and to be there consistently. Not to break the user down and rebuild them; the user has already committed to the work. The app just has to show up too.

Every copy decision, every micro-interaction, every session-end moment should ask: *does this reinforce the grinder's investment in the grind?* If yes, ship it. If no, rewrite.

## Reference material for Phase 2 designer / prompt engineer

If commissioning or prompting for the Fletcher character:

- **Framings**: portrait, seated three-quarter, or waist-up. Not a mugshot; not a glare.
- **Palette**: warm dark (amber, oxblood, muted olive) — not the cold blue-gray of *Whiplash*'s jazz club. This is a warmer room.
- **Emotional register**: present, focused, patient. Ready to work. Not raging, not smiling.
- **Age**: early-to-mid 50s.
- **Details that read as "coach"**: black turtleneck, guitar on a stand behind him, single warm lamp, exposed brick or wood-paneled wall.
- **Details to avoid**: any smile (too warm — reads as fake), any glare (too cold — reads as intimidating), any music-competition trophies (wrong archetype).
